Session 30: Key signatures from a mark list

Why. The Configurator's key-signature designer (its session 47, the operator-approved design docs/planning/key-signature-designer-design.md §9 in the Configurator repository) lets a designer choose a signature once for every key: scale notes with their own looks, four stacking rules, gold stems between the stacks, and the signature on the staff's middle, its bottom or top line set, or every line set; later, traditional sharps or flats. The engine's 'chord style draws only today's two-column stack. Configurator session 48 computes every other signature as an explicit list of marks (CNBKeySignature.marks), so the engine needs the mark-list hook this repository's backlog §2.3 says such signatures need (docs/planning/configurator-coordination-backlog.md). Configurator session 49 exports it and its engine round trip waits on this session.

The key's identity is settled: Configurator session 48's probe read, from real \key events with an engraver acknowledging each KeySignature grob, the context's tonic (note name and alteration) and the seven pitch classes from keyAlterations, for F♯ and G♭ major (one set of pitch classes, two spellings), B♭ major, a key change, the signature repeated at a system start and a \transposed score.

This adds a style and changes no existing output.

  1. Register.
  2. The setting. cnb:keysig-style 'marks and cnb:keysig-marks, a list with one entry per key. An entry's key is the tonic's note name and alteration (as \key gives them) and the key's sorted pitch classes, so C major and A minor are told apart by their tonic, and F♯ and G♭ major by their spelling. An entry gives its columns (each one's x offset in staff spaces from the signature's start), its bars (each one's x, its two ends in engine semitones, its colour and thickness in staff spaces) and its marks (each one's engine semitone, middle C = 0, its column, and either head with the shape, hollow and colour names Section A registers, a sized variant where it has a size, or sharp or flat). Two marks at one semitone in neighbouring columns are a double notehead and need nothing of their own. The engine computes no layout: the offsets are the Configurator's.
  3. Drawing. The key-signature engraver draws the entry for the score's \key: each head through its registered stencil, with each head column's ledger lines beneath it, sharps and flats from Emmentaler (accidentals.sharp, accidentals.flat) with no ledgers, and the bars beneath the marks. A key change draws the new key's marks and no cancelling naturals. A key not in the table draws no signature, and the engine warns naming it; it does not fall back to the scale stack. 'chord and 'none are unchanged.
  4. The space around it. Measure the space before the signature and after its last column with the mark list, for two, three and four columns, and give it in the handoff, so the Configurator's preview puts the first note where the engraving does (its 0.75, 3.5 and 4.8636 staff spaces are measured for 'chord's two columns).
  5. Tests. Unit and integration specs for a key change, an enharmonic pair, a transposed score, the signature at each system start, a double notehead, three and four columns with their bars, sharps and flats, and a key the table lacks; goldens and integration specs on LilyPond 2.24 and 2.26. Existing goldens stay byte-identical.
  6. A README section, a CHANGELOG.md 1.4.0 section, and version.json to 1.4.0: a minor release, a setting added and no existing output changed.
